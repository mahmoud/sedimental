It's been one week since FinFam's launch, and so far so good!

[FinFam's Show HN post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45252031) trended nicely, netting us thousands of new uniques.
Enough eyeballs to make me quite sure that if someone else had built a collaborative financial planner with a marketplace of interactive expert opinions, someone would've said something.

Especially gratified from the users I'm meeting _through_ the product.
Nothing like it.
If you're a new user and we haven't been acquainted yet, [let's chat](https://finfam.app/about)!

So, no regrets, right?
I've got one regret, and that's the subject of today's PSA.
[**Product Hunt**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_Hunt).

FWIW, I wasn't planning this post, and PH wasn't even much of a launch priority for FinFam.
But after seeing what I saw, I knew I had to bump this one up the queue.

After all, [my launch post on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7373298636744617984/)
mentioned our ProductHunt launch.
And now I'm cringing thinking about how I even sent an email out to a few product-oriented friends with a link to our post on ProductHunt.

Hours later I would realize that Product Hunt is, sadly, no more.
At least not the Product Hunt I knew from my days on [Stripe Invoicing](https://www.producthunt.com/products/stripe/launches/stripe-invoicing).
What's left is a husk, a zombie platform.
Active in appearance alone.

## I missed the memo

Turns out this has been happening for a while.
Just last year, Fabian Maume asked, ["Is Product Hunt Dying?"](https://www.tetriz.io/blog/is-product-hunt-dying/)
He's got lots of data and background, so I'll stick to filling in the now-obvious answer:
**Yes. Product Hunt is dead.**

And Fabian's not alone.
A quick search will [reveal][web-designer] [dozens][saas-analysis] of [nails][startup-value] in [the][hacker-news] [coffin][creative-coder].
I guess that's inevitable when the founder exits and in 2022 [a16z merges your mature platform](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-prologue/) with a crypto venture that no one remembers.

But how does a dead platform appear to function?

## The Zombie Grift

What really happens when you launch on Product Hunt?

ProductHunt has a weird quirk where it starts at midnight Pacific time.
Much like with Hacker News, the first few hours of a posting determine its longevity in the cycle.
But due to its fixed daily scheduling idiosyncrasy, a platform originating in Silicon Valley is unlikely to have its front page content by anyone in the American hemisphere.

Instead, Europe, APAC, and in particular India have an outsized influence.
This leads to a disconnect between the products featured and the community the platform was originally built to serve.

### 2. Gamification Gone Wrong

Product Hunt's gamified system of upvotes was once its greatest strength.
Now, it is its greatest weakness.
The system has been thoroughly gamed.
Vote spam is rampant.
Products from "studios" with large followings dominate, regardless of quality.
Genuine new products from independent makers struggle to be seen.

### 3. The "Featured" Black Box

Another major issue is the opaque "featured" system.
Only featured products are shown on the mobile app.
There is no indication before launch whether your product will be featured or not.
Even the "randomized" front page seems to heavily prioritize featured products.
This lack of transparency makes launching on Product Hunt a gamble.
You can spend weeks preparing, rally your community, and still end up invisible.

## Can Product Hunt be revived? Should it?

I suspect this decline was inevitable.
When founders exit and platforms are acquired, the original vision often fades.
There are alternatives, but it's not clear if any are worthwhile:

- [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/)
- [Betalist](https://betalist.com/)
- [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/)
- [Pitchwall](https://pitchwall.co/)
- [Side Projectors](https://www.sideprojectors.com/)
- [uneed.best](http://uneed.best/)

They're all nominally free, and when you're just launching, it probably doesn't hurt to have the extra SEO.


With many of these, the community element seems anemic.
Something about the focus on the new nets a fast flow of products and builders.
This erodes at the core community over time.
Indie Hackers is a notable exception because its members are united by more than just the idea of a product or a launch.

My favorite is still alternativeto.net, as its mission is to catalog all software, not just the newest.

## Goodbye ProductHunt

I suspect we won't see the likes of you again.
It was fun while it lasted.
